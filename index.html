2. My warning sign: working alone too long

When I reviewed the portfolio, one pattern stood out. Almost everything in it, I built alone. In Week 2 I planned to ask someone to try each project, and in Week 4 I wrote that my "next step" was to ask a family member to test safebackup. I still haven't done it. The peer review section is waiting for a teammate, and until this week the only person who had tested the website was me.

Working alone feels efficient, and some of it comes from good things: taking ownership, and not wanting to burden other people. But when I hold on too long, three things happen. I miss problems that another person would have seen in minutes. Small problems grow before anyone knows about them. And slowly I start relying only on my own understanding, which Proverbs 3:5–6 warns against.

President Kusch (Kusch & Kusch, 2024) taught that real understanding comes from more than one source. It combines knowledge, experience, and wisdom with the help of the Holy Ghost, and it depends on study and prayer together. He also cautioned us to be careful about the sources we rely on. When I work alone too long, I've made myself my only source. Sister Kusch's counsel was simple: if you lack something, ask the Lord for it. Asking is part of faith, not a sign that it's weak.

Early signals I now watch for

  • I've been stuck on a problem for more than two hours and haven't told anyone.
  • I catch myself thinking, "I'll show it to someone when it's finished."
  • My prayers turn into reports of what I did, instead of requests for help and direction.
  • I skip my ten minutes of scripture study because "there's too much to do."
  • I feel a little defensive when someone offers help or points out a problem.

Usability testing and design enhancement report

My portfolio is meant to show employers and my instructor how I work, and it should work for everyone who visits it. That includes people on a phone, people who use a keyboard or a screen reader, and people who prefer dark mode. Treating a visitor's time and access as worth protecting is the same stewardship I try to build into my software.

1. Testing questions

  1. Can a visitor find any section quickly, on both a computer and a phone?
  2. Does the site meet WCAG 2.2 level AA (World Wide Web Consortium [W3C], 2023) for contrast, keyboard use, and structure?
  3. Does the design support the content, or does it get in the way?

Week 5 · Task 2 · Ethical dilemma reflection

Reflecting on Equifax: my view and how I'll act

In Week 2 I studied the 2017 Equifax breach, and in Week 4 I designed a better way to handle it (see Ethics). This reflection is more personal. What do I think about it, and what would I do if I faced the same kind of choice?

My view of the dilemma

When I first read about Equifax, I saw it as a technical failure: a known vulnerability that wasn't patched. The more I studied it, the more I saw it as a failure of ownership and honesty. The warning came out in March 2017, and the systems still weren't fixed months later. According to the FTC (2019), Equifax's patching process and its inventory of systems weren't good enough to catch the gap. Then, after the attack was discovered in late July, about six weeks passed before the public was told (Equifax, 2017).

I don't believe every person involved was dishonest. I think the harder truth is that ordinary people in ordinary jobs each assumed someone else had it covered. That troubles me more, because it's easy to imagine myself in that position. I know from my years as a network administrator how easy it is to put off a patch when the system is busy and everything seems fine. That's not the same as neglecting it on purpose, but for 147 million people the result is the same.

On disclosure, my view has become more balanced. A company does need some time to understand what happened, and announcing wrong facts can cause harm too. But the people whose data was taken had the most to lose from waiting. They couldn't freeze their credit or watch for fraud until they knew. I believe the right approach is to tell people early what is confirmed and what is still unknown, and then keep updating them. Waiting until the story is complete puts the company's comfort ahead of the people it's accountable to.

How I'll handle similar situations

I won't face a breach of 147 million records every week. I will face small versions of the same choice. These are the ones I expect, and what I've decided ahead of time:

SituationWhat I'll do
I'm asked to mark a patch "done" before it's verified.Mark it "applied, not yet verified." I'll do the check that day or explain in writing why I can't.
A critical patch can't be applied in time because the system is too important to restart.Escalate it in writing with a temporary protection (a firewall rule or isolating the system) and a date. I won't leave it quietly open.
I'm asked to delay telling users about a problem that affects them.Ask why, and suggest a short, honest notice of what we know. If I'm asked to say something false, I won't, and I'll take it to the next level of leadership or the proper reporting channel.
I find a colleague's mistake.Talk to them first, privately and kindly, then help fix it. The goal is to fix the problem, not to blame the person.
I find my own mistake.Report it right away with what I know and what I'm doing about it, like publishing safebackup's weaknesses instead of hiding them.

Behind those answers is a short process I can use in the moment: stop and name it ("this is an ethical choice, not just a technical one"), get the facts, ask who could be hurt, act or escalate in writing, and tell the truth early.

What this exercise taught me about ethics in IT

  • Most ethical choices in IT look boring. A ticket status, a log entry, a default setting. Luke 16:10 fits exactly: faithfulness in small things is what makes a person trustworthy with big ones.
  • The people affected are usually invisible. No consumer was in the room when a patch was postponed. Part of my job is to keep those people in mind for them.
  • Good processes make honesty easier. In a crisis, people do what their routines allow. That's why I built the checklist and the notice template.
  • I can disagree without contention. Escalating a risk means disagreeing with someone, maybe my boss. The Savior taught that contention is not of Him (3 Nephi 11:29). I can raise a concern firmly and respectfully, focusing on the problem and not the person (Acree, 2021; Ensign College, n.d.).

Insights and realizations

1. Understanding comes from more than one source

President Kusch described understanding as knowledge, experience, and wisdom joined with the help of the Holy Ghost, through both study and prayer (Kusch & Kusch, 2024). That's also how good troubleshooting works. I read the documentation, check the logs, ask someone with more experience, and then decide. If I skip a source, I get a partial answer. In both my faith and my work, depending only on myself leaves something out.

2. Standing in truth is a daily, technical habit

Sister Marriott's message to stand in truth (Marriott, n.d.) made me think about how often my work asks me to state what's true: "the backup worked," "the server is patched," "your data is safe." Each statement either is or isn't true. Standing in truth at work means I only make claims I can verify, and I say "I don't know yet" when that's the honest answer.

3. The temple teaches order, and order serves people

The Lord's instruction to "establish a house… a house of order" (D&C 88:119) connects closely to what a systems administrator does. Inventories, documentation, change control, and backups are forms of order. But I realised the purpose of order is not control. In the temple, order exists so that people can come unto Christ. In IT, order exists so that people can rely on their systems and get on with their lives.

4. Differences make the work stronger

My work involves people who know less about technology than I do and people who know much more. I want both kinds of difference to make me better, not to cause contention. The guidance on embracing differences (Ensign College, n.d.) helped me see that the users who struggle with my tools are giving me the most useful feedback.

5. Patience is part of craftsmanship

"In your patience possess ye your souls" (Luke 21:19). This week's design work was slow and step by step: test, fix one thing, test again. I used to see that as a delay. Now I see it as the same patience the Lord shows with me as He shapes my character a step at a time.

My commitment

I want to be the kind of IT professional people can trust without checking up on me, because I've already checked. And when I'm stuck, I want to ask for help early and humbly. I'll measure my progress by the strategies in my review: did I ask, did I verify, and did I serve the person on the other side of the system?

Week 4 · Task 3 · Ethical dilemma alternative

Equifax 2017: a better way to handle it

In Week 2 I analysed the 2017 Equifax breach. About 147 million people's names, birth dates, Social Security numbers, and addresses were exposed through an Apache Struts vulnerability. The FTC (2019) alleged that the company was warned in March 2017 and did not patch it. Equifax (2017) reported detecting suspicious traffic on 29 July, and it announced the breach publicly on 7 September. That's about six weeks later. The settlement reached at least US$575 million.

Essential ethical principles

PrincipleWhat it requires hereWhere it was weakest
Responsibility (stewardship)Whoever holds people's data must actively protect it, with a named owner for every critical risk.A known critical vulnerability was not fixed and verified.
Honesty and transparencyTell affected people the confirmed facts promptly, and say what is still uncertain.Public notice came weeks after detection.
FairnessConsumers didn't choose to be in the database, so they shouldn't carry the cost of the failure.Consumers had to act on their own to protect themselves.
AccountabilityDecisions and failures can be traced to people and processes that can be corrected.The FTC alleged gaps in patching and in the asset inventory.

The alternative approach

My alternative works at two points: before the breach (prevention) and after detection (response). Both rest on the principles above.

1. Before: treat a critical advisory as a stewardship task with an owner

  • Keep a current asset inventory so that "Where do we run Apache Struts?" has an answer within minutes.
  • Assign every critical advisory a named owner and a deadline (for example 48 hours), using the patch verification checklist in my Projects.
  • Require evidence the fix worked, such as a version check and a re-scan, and a second person to verify it. A ticket marked "done" isn't proof.
  • If a system can't be patched in time, escalate it to leadership with compensating controls (a web application firewall rule, or isolating the system). Don't leave it silently exposed.

2. After detection: contain fast and tell the truth early

  • Contain immediately and preserve the logs. Equifax did take the application offline on 30 July.
  • Notify regulators and affected people as soon as the scope is reasonably confirmed. Use staged updates that separate "what we know" from "what we're still investigating", instead of waiting for a complete picture.
  • Give free, automatic protections (credit freezes and monitoring) without requiring people to give up legal rights, and warn them about phishing that imitates the company's own notices.
  • Hold a blameless but accountable post-incident review. Fix the process, publish what changed, and name who owns each fix.

Why this is better for everyone involved

StakeholderOutcome under the alternative
ConsumersThe breach most likely never happens, because a critical patch was verified within days. If it did happen, people could freeze credit weeks sooner, which shortens the window when stolen data is most useful to fraudsters.
IT and security staffClear ownership and escalation protect individuals. Raising a risk is part of the job, not a career risk, and nobody quietly carries blame for a process gap.
Leadership and shareholdersPatching costs a fraction of a US$575–700 million settlement. Fast honest disclosure also limits the loss of trust that follows when people feel misled.
Regulators and the publicTimely, factual notice lets authorities coordinate the response and strengthens trust in the credit system as a whole.

The alternative is better because it turns values into routines. Honesty and responsibility are hardest to practise in the middle of a crisis, and much easier when a checklist already says who acts, by when, and what proof is required. Luke 16:10 describes this exactly: being faithful in the small step of verifying one patch is what makes it possible to be trusted with the data of 147 million people.

2. Ethical analysis

  • Data privacy. Backups multiply copies of data. Every copy of a private key is another place it can leak from. Copying secrets silently fails the user.
  • Honesty. A tool that says "success" without evidence is making a claim it can't back up. That is the same failure I criticised in the Equifax case, where "patched" wasn't verified.
  • Informed consent. Users should knowingly choose risky behaviour, such as including secrets or deleting old backups. It shouldn't happen to them by default.
  • Accessibility and clarity. Error messages should be readable by a non-programmer. Output should work with screen readers, so it uses plain text and doesn't depend on colour.
  • Logging ethics. Logs are useful, but they shouldn't record file contents, secret file names, or the user's home folder path, which contains their name.

4. Decision-making process

I ranked each problem by who gets hurt and how badly, not by how interesting it was to code. Silent data loss and leaked secrets came first. Convenience features came last. I deliberately left encryption out of this version. Doing it properly needs a third-party library and careful key management, and a rushed implementation would give false security, which is the very thing this refinement is meant to remove. Instead, I documented it as a known limitation and planned it for version 3.

I kept the tool to the Python standard library so anyone can run it without installing packages. That's a small decision about accessibility and about the security of the supply chain.

safebackup.py (version 2, refined)
"""safebackup.py — version 2 (refined).

A small, standard-library-only backup tool built for trustworthy results:

  backup   Copy a folder into a timestamped snapshot with a SHA-256 manifest.
  verify   Re-hash a snapshot and report any missing, changed, or extra files.
  restore  Verify a snapshot, then restore it to a new or empty folder.
  prune    Show (or, with --apply, delete) snapshots beyond a retention limit.

Design principles (see REFINEMENT_REPORT.md):
  * Honesty     - a backup is only reported as successful after it is verified.
  * Stewardship - likely secrets are excluded unless the user explicitly opts in;
                  logs never record file contents or the user's home path.
  * Safety      - snapshots are written atomically; restores never overwrite
                  existing files or write outside the chosen folder;
                  deletion is a dry run unless --apply is given.
  * Clarity     - plain-language messages and documented exit codes.

Author: Gaston Kasongo (IT 497). Uses only the Python standard library.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import fnmatch
import hashlib
import json
import os
import shutil
import sys
from datetime import datetime, timezone
from pathlib import Path, PurePosixPath

VERSION = "2.0"
MANIFEST = "manifest.json"
LOG_FILE = "safebackup.log.jsonl"
SNAPSHOT_PREFIX = "snapshot_"

# Exit codes (documented in README so scripts and schedulers can react).
EXIT_OK = 0
EXIT_USAGE = 2          # bad arguments, missing or invalid folders
EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED = 3  # integrity problem detected
EXIT_UNSAFE = 4         # refused an unsafe action (overwrite, path escape)
EXIT_NO_SPACE = 5       # not enough free space

# Files that commonly hold credentials or private keys. Excluded by default.
SENSITIVE_PATTERNS = [
    ".env", ".env.*", "*.pem", "*.key", "*.pfx", "*.p12", "id_rsa", "id_rsa*",
    "id_ed25519*", "*.kdbx", "credentials*", "*secret*", ".git-credentials",
    ".netrc", "*.ovpn",
]
ALWAYS_SKIP = [MANIFEST, LOG_FILE, "__pycache__", "*.partial"]


class BackupError(Exception):
    """An expected failure with a plain-language message and exit code."""

    def __init__(self, message: str, code: int = EXIT_USAGE):
        super().__init__(message)
        self.code = code


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- helpers
def redact(path: Path | str) -> str:
    """Replace the user's home folder with '~' so logs do not expose names."""
    text = str(path)
    home = str(Path.home())
    return "~" + text[len(home):] if home and text.startswith(home) else text


def sha256_of(path: Path, chunk: int = 1024 * 1024) -> str:
    digest = hashlib.sha256()
    with open(path, "rb") as handle:
        for block in iter(lambda: handle.read(chunk), b""):
            digest.update(block)
    return digest.hexdigest()


def matches(name: str, patterns: list[str]) -> bool:
    lower = name.lower()
    return any(fnmatch.fnmatch(lower, p.lower()) for p in patterns)


def log_event(dest: Path, event: dict) -> None:
    """Append one JSON line to the destination log (metadata only)."""
    event = {"time": datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat(timespec="seconds"),
             "version": VERSION, **event}
    with open(dest / LOG_FILE, "a", encoding="utf-8") as handle:
        handle.write(json.dumps(event) + "\n")


def say(message: str, quiet: bool = False) -> None:
    if not quiet:
        print(message)


def snapshots(dest: Path) -> list[Path]:
    """Completed snapshots (those with a manifest), oldest first."""
    found = [p for p in dest.iterdir()
             if p.is_dir() and p.name.startswith(SNAPSHOT_PREFIX)
             and not p.name.endswith(".partial") and (p / MANIFEST).is_file()]
    return sorted(found, key=lambda p: p.name)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- collect
def collect(source: Path, include_sensitive: bool, extra_excludes: list[str]):
    """Walk the source without following symlinks.

    Returns (files, skipped) where files is a list of relative POSIX paths and
    skipped counts the reasons files were left out.
    """
    files: list[str] = []
    skipped = {"sensitive": [], "symlink": 0, "excluded": 0, "unreadable": 0}
    patterns = list(extra_excludes)
    for root, dirs, names in os.walk(source, followlinks=False):
        root_path = Path(root)
        kept_dirs = []
        for d in dirs:
            p = root_path / d
            if p.is_symlink():
                skipped["symlink"] += 1
            elif matches(d, ALWAYS_SKIP + patterns):
                skipped["excluded"] += 1
            else:
                kept_dirs.append(d)
        dirs[:] = sorted(kept_dirs)
        for name in sorted(names):
            p = root_path / name
            rel = p.relative_to(source).as_posix()
            if p.is_symlink():
                skipped["symlink"] += 1
            elif matches(name, ALWAYS_SKIP + patterns):
                skipped["excluded"] += 1
            elif not include_sensitive and matches(name, SENSITIVE_PATTERNS):
                skipped["sensitive"].append(rel)
            elif not os.access(p, os.R_OK):
                skipped["unreadable"] += 1
            else:
                files.append(rel)
    return files, skipped


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- commands
def cmd_backup(args) -> int:
    source = Path(args.source).expanduser().resolve()
    dest = Path(args.dest).expanduser().resolve()
    if not source.is_dir():
        raise BackupError(f"The source folder was not found: {redact(source)}. "
                          "Check the spelling and try again.")
    if dest == source or source in dest.parents:
        raise BackupError("The destination cannot be inside the source folder; "
                          "the backup would copy itself.", EXIT_UNSAFE)
    dest.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)

    files, skipped = collect(source, args.include_sensitive, args.exclude or [])
    total = sum((source / f).stat().st_size for f in files)
    free = shutil.disk_usage(dest).free
    if total > free:
        raise BackupError(f"Not enough free space: need {total:,} bytes, "
                          f"{free:,} available.", EXIT_NO_SPACE)

    stamp = datetime.now().strftime("%Y%m%d_%H%M%S_%f")
    final = dest / f"{SNAPSHOT_PREFIX}{stamp}"
    partial = dest / f"{final.name}.partial"
    partial.mkdir()
    entries = []
    try:
        for rel in files:
            src = source / rel
            out = partial / PurePosixPath(rel)
            out.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
            shutil.copy2(src, out)
            entries.append({"path": rel, "size": out.stat().st_size,
                            "sha256": sha256_of(out),
                            "source_sha256": sha256_of(src)})
        mismatched = [e["path"] for e in entries
                      if e["sha256"] != e.pop("source_sha256")]
        if mismatched:
            raise BackupError("Copied files did not match the originals "
                              f"(possibly changed during backup): {mismatched[:5]}",
                              EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED)
        manifest = {"tool": "safebackup", "version": VERSION,
                    "created": datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat(timespec="seconds"),
                    "source": redact(source), "file_count": len(entries),
                    "total_bytes": total, "files": entries,
                    "skipped": {"sensitive_count": len(skipped["sensitive"]),
                                "symlinks": skipped["symlink"],
                                "excluded": skipped["excluded"],
                                "unreadable": skipped["unreadable"]}}
        (partial / MANIFEST).write_text(json.dumps(manifest, indent=2), "utf-8")
        partial.rename(final)  # atomic on the same file system
    except BaseException:
        shutil.rmtree(partial, ignore_errors=True)
        raise

    log_event(dest, {"action": "backup", "result": "ok", "snapshot": final.name,
                     "files": len(entries), "bytes": total,
                     "sensitive_skipped": len(skipped["sensitive"])})
    say(f"Backup complete and verified: {len(entries)} files "
        f"({total:,} bytes) saved to {final.name}.", args.quiet)
    if skipped["sensitive"]:
        say(f"Left out {len(skipped['sensitive'])} file(s) that look like "
            "passwords or keys:", args.quiet)
        for rel in skipped["sensitive"][:10]:
            say(f"  - {rel}", args.quiet)
        say("  Store secrets in a password manager, or rerun with "
            "--include-sensitive if you are sure.", args.quiet)
    if skipped["symlink"] or skipped["unreadable"]:
        say(f"Also skipped {skipped['symlink']} shortcut(s)/symlink(s) and "
            f"{skipped['unreadable']} unreadable file(s).", args.quiet)
    return EXIT_OK


def verify_snapshot(snapshot: Path) -> dict:
    manifest_path = snapshot / MANIFEST
    if not manifest_path.is_file():
        raise BackupError(f"{snapshot.name} has no manifest; it may be "
                          "incomplete or not a safebackup snapshot.",
                          EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED)
    manifest = json.loads(manifest_path.read_text("utf-8"))
    listed = {e["path"]: e for e in manifest["files"]}
    missing, changed = [], []
    for rel, entry in listed.items():
        p = snapshot / PurePosixPath(rel)
        if not p.is_file():
            missing.append(rel)
        elif sha256_of(p) != entry["sha256"]:
            changed.append(rel)
    present = {p.relative_to(snapshot).as_posix()
               for p in snapshot.rglob("*") if p.is_file()} - {MANIFEST}
    extra = sorted(present - set(listed))
    return {"manifest": manifest, "missing": missing,
            "changed": changed, "extra": extra}


def resolve_snapshot(dest: Path, name: str | None) -> Path:
    if not dest.is_dir():
        raise BackupError(f"Backup folder not found: {redact(dest)}")
    if name in (None, "latest"):
        found = snapshots(dest)
        if not found:
            raise BackupError("No completed snapshots were found.")
        return found[-1]
    snap = (dest / name).resolve()
    if snap.parent != dest or not snap.is_dir():
        raise BackupError(f"Snapshot not found: {name}")
    return snap


def cmd_verify(args) -> int:
    dest = Path(args.dest).expanduser().resolve()
    snap = resolve_snapshot(dest, args.snapshot)
    result = verify_snapshot(snap)
    ok = not (result["missing"] or result["changed"] or result["extra"])
    log_event(dest, {"action": "verify", "snapshot": snap.name,
                     "result": "ok" if ok else "failed",
                     "missing": len(result["missing"]),
                     "changed": len(result["changed"]),
                     "extra": len(result["extra"])})
    if ok:
        say(f"{snap.name}: all {result['manifest']['file_count']} files "
            "match their recorded checksums.", args.quiet)
        return EXIT_OK
    print(f"{snap.name}: verification FAILED.")
    for label in ("missing", "changed", "extra"):
        for rel in result[label][:10]:
            print(f"  {label}: {rel}")
    return EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED


def cmd_restore(args) -> int:
    dest = Path(args.dest).expanduser().resolve()
    snap = resolve_snapshot(dest, args.snapshot)
    target = Path(args.target).expanduser().resolve()
    result = verify_snapshot(snap)
    if result["missing"] or result["changed"]:
        raise BackupError(f"{snap.name} failed verification, so nothing was "
                          "restored. Run 'verify' for details.", EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED)
    if target.exists() and any(target.iterdir()) and not args.overwrite:
        raise BackupError("The restore folder is not empty. Choose an empty "
                          "folder, or add --overwrite to replace matching files.",
                          EXIT_UNSAFE)
    restored = 0
    for entry in result["manifest"]["files"]:
        rel = PurePosixPath(entry["path"])
        out = (target / rel).resolve()
        if rel.is_absolute() or ".." in rel.parts or target not in out.parents:
            raise BackupError(f"Refused unsafe path in manifest: {rel}", EXIT_UNSAFE)
        out.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
        shutil.copy2(snap / rel, out)
        if sha256_of(out) != entry["sha256"]:
            raise BackupError(f"Restored copy of {rel} did not match its checksum.",
                              EXIT_VERIFY_FAILED)
        restored += 1
    log_event(dest, {"action": "restore", "snapshot": snap.name,
                     "result": "ok", "files": restored})
    say(f"Restored and verified {restored} files from {snap.name}.", args.quiet)
    return EXIT_OK


def cmd_prune(args) -> int:
    dest = Path(args.dest).expanduser().resolve()
    if args.keep < 1:
        raise BackupError("--keep must be at least 1 so one backup always remains.")
    found = snapshots(dest)
    doomed = found[:-args.keep] if len(found) > args.keep else []
    if not doomed:
        say(f"Nothing to remove: {len(found)} snapshot(s), keeping {args.keep}.",
            args.quiet)
        return EXIT_OK
    verb = "Removing" if args.apply else "Would remove (dry run)"
    for snap in doomed:
        say(f"{verb}: {snap.name}", args.quiet)
        if args.apply:
            shutil.rmtree(snap)
    if not args.apply:
        say("No files were deleted. Add --apply to delete these snapshots.",
            args.quiet)
    log_event(dest, {"action": "prune", "applied": args.apply,
                     "removed": len(doomed) if args.apply else 0,
                     "candidates": len(doomed)})
    return EXIT_OK

def build_parser() -> argparse.ArgumentParser:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(
        prog="safebackup",
        description="Verified, privacy-aware folder backups.")
    parser.add_argument("--version", action="version", version=VERSION)
    parser.add_argument("-q", "--quiet", action="store_true",
                        help="only print errors")
    sub = parser.add_subparsers(dest="command", required=True)

    b = sub.add_parser("backup", help="create a verified snapshot")
    b.add_argument("source"), b.add_argument("dest")
    b.add_argument("--exclude", action="append", metavar="PATTERN",
                   help="extra file/folder pattern to skip (repeatable)")
    b.add_argument("--include-sensitive", action="store_true",
                   help="also copy files that look like passwords or keys")
    b.set_defaults(func=cmd_backup)

    v = sub.add_parser("verify", help="check a snapshot against its manifest")
    v.add_argument("dest"), v.add_argument("--snapshot", default="latest")
    v.set_defaults(func=cmd_verify)

    r = sub.add_parser("restore", help="restore a verified snapshot")
    r.add_argument("dest"), r.add_argument("target")
    r.add_argument("--snapshot", default="latest")
    r.add_argument("--overwrite", action="store_true",
                   help="allow restoring into a non-empty folder")
    r.set_defaults(func=cmd_restore)

    p = sub.add_parser("prune", help="apply a retention limit")
    p.add_argument("dest"), p.add_argument("--keep", type=int, default=7)
    p.add_argument("--apply", action="store_true",
                   help="actually delete (default is a dry run)")
    p.set_defaults(func=cmd_prune)
    return parser


def main(argv: list[str] | None = None) -> int:
    args = build_parser().parse_args(argv)
    try:
        return args.func(args)
    except BackupError as err:
        print(f"Error: {err}", file=sys.stderr)
        return err.code
    except PermissionError as err:
        print(f"Error: permission denied for {redact(err.filename or '')}.",
              file=sys.stderr)
        return EXIT_USAGE
